02

Core components

Three decisions carry most of the engine's behaviour. Each one exists because a cheaper design was tried first and measured.

Slow clock

DLMM_LOOP

*/30 * * * *

Scans 600 pools, runs the anti-rug screen, and spends one deepseek-reasoner call to pick an entry. This clock governs only whether a new position may be opened.

Why 30 minutes, not 10

Chain-of-thought dominates the token bill. At 10 minutes the screener burned three times the tokens for entries the anti-churn gates mostly refuse anyway — the pool cooldown is measured in hours, so a 10-minute cadence was re-asking a question whose answer could not have changed.

  • · Rate-limited upstreams live here
  • · The only LLM cost in the system
  • · Skips its own monitor stage while the fast monitor is on
Fast clock

FAST_MONITOR

* * * * *

Marks every open position against the live Meteora price, accrues fees, and fires exits. It carries none of the screener's rate-limited upstreams and none of its LLM cost, which is exactly why it can run every 60 seconds.

Why a stop-loss needs its own clock

An exit threshold is only as tight as the interval that tests it. On the 10-minute clock the dry run overshot a −8% stop all the way to −13.84%. Slowing the screener must never slow this loop.

  • · Uses tryRun — skips when the mutex is contended
  • · Prices from Meteora, never DexScreener
  • · Fee accrual clamped to 1h after downtime

The one rule that ties them together

Fee accrual is rate × (now − last_checked_at). Two overlapping passes would measure from the same stored timestamp and book the same interval twice, and both could read a row as ACTIVE and close it independently. The per-job withLock wrappers only stop a job overlapping itself; cross-clock safety is positionMutex. Nothing that does I/O may hold it — Telegram sends and post-mortem LLM calls are queued and settled after release, or a DeepSeek round-trip would silently void the 60-second exit guarantee.

Token budget: a fixed ceiling, not a retry ladder

deepseek-reasoner writes its chain-of-thought before it writes the answer, so max_tokens covers both. A chat-sized budget is spent thinking, and the answer never begins.

REASONER_MAX_TOKENS 16,000
chain-of-thought (>8,000 observed) answer headroom
The bug it replaced

The budget used to double on a truncated attempt (16k → 32k), so a single tick could pay 48,000 reasoning tokens and still return nothing. The failure mode billed twice over.

Why not lower it

16,000 is measured, not round. At 4k/8k every cycle burned a wasted first attempt and long CoT still hit the cap. Since truncation now means a skipped cycle rather than a bigger retry, cutting this would simply stop the engine opening positions.

Graceful skip

A reasoner that burns its budget on thinking and returns nothing is an expected outcome, not a fault. There is no decision to act on, and no amount of retrying inside one tick produces one.

DeepSeekTruncatedError
↳ skip this cycle
↳ open positions unaffected
↳ fast monitor still exits them

Only truncation is swallowed. Any other DeepSeek failure propagates, because it means something is genuinely wrong.

The gates are the edge

Re-running the identical 212-day window with the anti-churn gates switched off is the cleanest evidence in the whole harness. Same pools, same prices, same exit rules — only the gates change. Without them the engine trades 2.5× more often and turns a profit into a loss.

Cooldown
4 hours

A pool is benched after any close, win or lose. Blocked 144 candidate entries over the window.

Fails open: an unreadable timestamp is an expired bench, not a ban. A broken clock must not freeze the screener.

Lockout
2× / 24h

Two consecutive failing closes on one pool trip a 24-hour breaker. Blocked 263 entries — the single busiest gate of the two.

A timeout is neutral and resets the run. A manual /close_all is invisible to it — the breaker measures the pool, not the operator.

Breakeven gate
2.5× cost

Projected 24h fees must clear 2.5× the round-trip gas and slippage, or the pool is skipped. 8.3% of all rejections.

When the gas estimate is unavailable it assumes a conservative cost, never zero — treating the trip as free is what allowed the churn.

Universe bounds
$50k–$500k

TVL band plus a 48-hour minimum pool age and a 10% 1h surge cap. Together, 41% of all rejections.

Age is evaluated at the simulated bar, not today — a pool 200 days old now was three hours old in June.

The screen that fails closed

Unlike the cooldown, the anti-rug screen returns UNKNOWN — never PASS — when a check could not run, and an UNKNOWN is rejected. Mint and freeze authority are stored as 1 / 0 / null, where null means the check never ran; collapsing null to false would claim an authority is live when it was simply never checked. On the public Solana RPC, getTokenLargestAccounts is blocked, so every candidate is rejected and nothing opens. That is correct behaviour, not a bug.

Read this before quoting the numbers
The window is 212 days, not a year
The free data tier serves ~208 days of hourly OHLCV and answers HTTP 401 beyond it. A full year needs a paid key.
Every trade came from one cohort
All 47 landed on dying pools; the survivor cohort was offered 14 pools and took none. That is a selection artefact, not a property of the strategy.
The TVL assumption flips the sign
Fee income scales as 1/k. At the fitted p25 the same run loses −$23.91; at p75 it never trades. +19.39% is one draw from a range that crosses zero.
